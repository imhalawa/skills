# Voice Reference

Use this reference for long-form writing and revisions. It describes decisions,
not a persona to copy.

## Target

- **Direct:** open with the actual point. No ceremonial setup.
- **Concrete:** prefer an exact mechanism, small trace, or observable result to
  abstract framing.
- **Compact, not incomplete:** remove what does not help understanding or
  action; retain anything that changes correctness or a decision.
- **Plain and exact:** keep necessary technical terms and simplify the grammar
  around them.
- **Candid:** state that something is wrong, weak, uncertain, unnecessary, or
  overcomplicated when that is the useful judgment.
- **Natural:** sound like an engineer talking to another engineer, not a brand,
  lecturer, salesperson, or generic AI assistant.
- **Context-aware:** let a chat answer be short and a substantial article be
  long. Every paragraph still earns its place.

## Preserve authorship

For source-based revision, identify the author's claims, stance, rhythm, humor,
and deliberate rough edges before editing. Preserve them unless they block
understanding or conflict with the request. Tighten the technical path beneath
the personality; do not normalize the author into a neutral house style.

Do not imitate chat fragments, lowercase typing, spelling mistakes, or moments
of frustration. Do not imitate AI-generated material merely associated with the
author. If provenance is unclear, preserve facts and ask before claiming that a
style is the author's.

Use **semantic expansion**, not factual expansion: make the relationship among
supplied facts clearer without adding project history. A note that transactions
were split permits explaining the atomicity boundary in general; it does not
establish which messages, databases, incidents, teams, metrics, or tools existed.
Mark a useful extrapolation as a hypothetical example or omit it. When missing
details are essential, ask instead of completing the story from plausibility.

## Sentence and structure choices

- Prefer subjects that perform actions: “The client retries,” not “a retry is
  performed.”
- Use one term for one concept. Change a term only when the distinction changes.
- Keep the causal links explicit: because, therefore, unless, after, before.
- Use contrast when it sharpens a boundary, not to manufacture drama.
- Use first person when it carries ownership, experience, or epistemic limits.
- Let a strong final consequence end the piece. Do not append a generic recap or
  inspirational aphorism.

## Reject

- “In today's rapidly evolving landscape” and similar throat-clearing.
- Repeated three-part slogans, canned rhetorical questions, and fake quotations.
- Generic headings such as “Introduction,” “Key Takeaways,” or “Conclusion” when
  a descriptive heading or no heading is better.
- Dense nominalizations and status-heavy vocabulary.
- Lists that merely restate adjacent prose.
- Confidence unsupported by the source or evidence.
- A polished cadence that sounds impressive but changes the author's stance.
